# Working model
- **Checked:** Control is only `ACTION6(x,y)`. A tethered system has one rounded 5×5 player, N diamond endpoint blobs, and straight color-1 tethers. Its player coordinate is `floor(componentwise mean(endpoint centers))`; this has held for N=2, 3, and 4. Earlier levels used a color-6 player core; levels 5–6 instead have white/colorable players with no special core and color-4 endpoint centers.
- **Checked:** Exactly one endpoint globally is active, shown by a color-0 body. A normal field click relocates that active endpoint center exactly to the click, with no range limit, then recomputes its system. Clicking any body cell of an inactive color-3 endpoint only selects it (old active 0→3, selected 3→0). Thus changing endpoints normally costs select + destination.
- **Checked:** A player solves a matching dotted ring when it enters the ring interior; exact center is sufficient but not necessary. A solved ring flashes and settles with all 12 perimeter cells changed to 0; its assembly remains until the whole level completes.
- **Checked:** Players and rings can be multicolored, and directional color pattern matters. Every corresponding player/ring on a multi-player level must be solved; decoy rings may be present.
- **Checked:** Color 2 is wall terrain. The x=0 strip is a fresh 64-action timer per level, filling one cell 0→5 per action; it is not gameplay. Level 6's normal open background is color 5.
- **Strongly supported by every route, collision itself unprobed:** Require every endpoint/player shape and every exact Bresenham tether to avoid color-2 terrain, large terrain of other colors when present, and other assemblies. Small isolated colored cells can be ring parts. `scratch/geom.py` exactly reproduces all observed tether cells.
- **Checked planning arithmetic:** A final endpoint-coordinate sum in `[N*T, N*T+N-1]` per axis puts the floored mean at exact target T. Obstacles may force waypoint moves or moving a currently inactive endpoint first.
- **Checked on level 5:** A white player exactly overlapping a filled rounded 5×5 paint station acquires and carries its directional colored portion; when the player leaves, the station is consumed to background. Complementary portions combine, and the completed player solves the identically patterned dotted ring.
- **Supported by level-4 correspondence / level-6 layout:** Ring dots encode angular sectors and omit cardinal boundary directions. Level-6 paint masks for a target overlap only on sector boundaries, so collection order may affect ambiguous boundary cells but likely not matching; overwrite/first-fill behavior itself is still untested.
